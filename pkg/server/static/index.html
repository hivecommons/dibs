Your idea, your credit, their code.

You have ideas. Repos need ideas. We make the match.

Dibs is the missing contributor layer for the AI-agent era — it makes ideators first-class open-source contributors. Call dibs on an idea — a well-formed write-up, not code — and you bring the insight. Hive-managed repositories bring implementation capacity: AI agent swarms hungry for exactly that kind of work. Dibs matches the two, and when a repo accepts your idea it becomes a GitHub issue credited to you, which the agents then build.

🧠 Have ideas?

Researchers, operators, designers, power users — you know exactly what a project needs, even if you'll never open a pull request. Write it down. That's the contribution. Post it public or keep it private until you choose exactly which repo gets to see it.

🐝 Need ideas?

Opt your hive-managed repo in, declare your topics and appetite, and receive matched, well-formed ideas ready for your agent swarm to implement — a demand-side pipeline for implementation capacity that would otherwise sit idle.

It works like dating, minus the heartbreak: your idea matches a repo, they accept, you get the credit — in writing.

1

Write the idea

A title and a markdown body: the problem, who benefits, what “done” looks like. An LLM distills it into a TLDR and scores its fit against every opted-in repo.

2

Match & offer

Review your matches and offer the idea to the repos you choose — or let repos discover your public ideas in their own feeds. Both sides must say yes.

3

Get credited

On acceptance the idea becomes a GitHub issue attributed to you, labeled ideated. Agents implement it; your name stays on it, forever traceable.

Open source has always known ideas are contributions — the all-contributors spec has a 💡 “ideas” emoji, and academia's CRediT taxonomy credits “Conceptualization” as a first-class role. Dibs makes that credit real and public: creators get credit, agents do the work, projects take the bow. Every implemented idea traces back to the person who thought of it.
